# Tutorial Guide — Operation Zero
## Detective Files — Mission 20

- **Mission Title:** Operation Zero
- **Mission ID:** `case-000` (Mission 20)
- **Mission Purpose:** The player's first investigation. Teaches every core
  application and mechanic through natural play, with no external tutorial
  screens or modal walkthroughs.

---

## Story Introduction

You've just joined the Central Investigation Department. Your first
assignment lands on your desk from Captain Morgan: Danny Cole, a
stockroom clerk at a downtown pawn shop, never came home after closing up
alone last night. His car is still parked outside the shop, keys still in
the ignition.

It's a small case — which is exactly why it's yours.

## Player Role

You are a rookie detective operating **CID OS**, the department's
investigation workstation. Everything you need — mail, evidence, witness
contacts, maps, camera footage, the forensics lab, criminal records, and
your investigation board — lives on this one desktop.

## Starting Situation

You begin at the CID OS desktop with one new message waiting in Police
Mail: your assignment briefing from Captain Morgan.

## Main Objective

Figure out what happened to Danny Cole, build your case, and submit your
resolution to Headquarters.

---

## Tutorial Objectives

Operation Zero walks you through roughly a dozen small objectives, each
introducing exactly one new mechanic. You'll always be able to see your
current objective and what to do next in the **Active Investigation**
widget — you never need to guess what's expected of you.

1. Read your assignment
2. Review the evidence
3. Inspect the overturned stool
4. Interview the witness
5. Visit the crime scene
6. Check the camera footage
7. Inspect the torn pawn ticket
8. Request a forensic analysis
9. Collect the forensic report
10. Look up the suspect
11. Build your case on the board
12. Write up your theory

Two more are entirely optional, for players who want to dig deeper:
reviewing every piece of evidence, and pinning your key evidence for easy
reference.

## Step-by-Step Investigation Flow

1. **Open Police Mail.** Read Captain Morgan's briefing. It tells you what
   happened and points you toward the Evidence Database.
2. **Open the Evidence Database.** Everything logged from the scene is
   here. Inspect the overturned stool — the first sign something didn't
   go quietly.
3. **Open Messenger.** Danny's coworker, Elena Cruz, is willing to talk.
   Ask her what she remembers; her answers point you toward the scene
   itself and toward a name.
4. **Open the City Map.** Select the pawn shop to visit the scene directly.
5. **Open the CCTV Viewer.** The Back Alley Camera caught something —
   watch the footage.
6. **Back in the Evidence Database**, take a closer look at the torn pawn
   ticket. There's a name on it.
7. **Open the Forensics Lab.** Submit the cut zip tie for analysis. This
   takes a short while to process — you can keep working on other things
   while you wait, or just sit tight.
8. **Check back in the Forensics Lab** once the analysis is ready. It
   gives you a name.
9. **Open the Criminal Database.** Look up that name.
10. **Open the Investigation Board.** Connect the evidence, the witness,
    and your suspect.
11. **Add a theory card** summarizing what you believe happened.
12. **Submit your resolution.** Headquarters will let you know how you did.

## Applications Introduced

Operation Zero is the first time you'll touch every one of these:

- **Police Mail** — read briefings and updates from Headquarters.
- **Evidence Database** — inspect everything logged from a scene.
- **Messenger** — talk to witnesses.
- **City Map** — visit locations relevant to the case.
- **CCTV Viewer** — watch camera footage.
- **Forensics Lab** — request and collect lab analysis on physical evidence.
- **Criminal Database** — look up people of interest.
- **Investigation Board** — connect evidence and write up your theory.

## Evidence Introduced

Five items are logged from the scene: the case brief, an overturned
stool, a cut zip tie, a torn pawn ticket, and Danny's dropped phone. Not
every piece of evidence is essential — part of the job is telling what
matters from what doesn't.

## Witnesses Introduced

**Elena Cruz**, Danny's coworker, is the case's one witness. She was the
last person to see him before he closed up alone.

## Investigation Board Usage

The board is where loose threads become a case. Drag evidence and people
onto the board, connect them to show how they relate, and add a theory
card once you're confident you understand what happened. You'll want at
least a few evidence connections and a suspect in place before writing
your theory — a board with a single card rarely tells a convincing story.

## Active Investigation Usage

The Active Investigation widget, visible throughout the case, always
shows: your current objective, your overall progress, the current phase
of the investigation, and a suggested next action. If you're ever unsure
what to do, check there first.

## Objective System Explanation

Objectives appear one at a time as you make progress — you won't be
shown things before they're relevant. Most are required to move the case
forward; a couple are optional and just reward players who explore
further. If you get stuck for a while, Headquarters will send a reminder
email pointing you toward what's next.

## Case Progression

The investigation moves through phases: from your initial assignment,
through evidence review, witness contact, field work, the lab, a
background check, building your theory, and finally resolution. Each
phase naturally opens up the next application you'll need.

## How to Complete the Investigation

Once you've built your theory on the Investigation Board, submit your
resolution. You'll walk through a short series of choices — suspect,
method, location, motive, and rough timeline — before confirming.

## Final Resolution

Submit your resolution once you're confident in your theory. Headquarters
reviews it and lets you know whether your conclusion holds up.

## Tutorial Completion State

Once your resolution is submitted and confirmed, Operation Zero is
complete. The case moves to your solved cases list, and you're free to
take on your next assignment.

## What the Player Should Learn From This Mission

By the end of Operation Zero, you should be comfortable with:

- Reading mail and evidence, and knowing where each application lives
- Talking to a witness through Messenger
- Using the City Map and CCTV Viewer to gather field information
- Requesting and waiting on a forensics report
- Looking someone up in the Criminal Database
- Building a case on the Investigation Board and forming a theory
- Submitting a resolution and reading Headquarters' feedback

Nothing here is meant to be difficult — the goal is fluency with the
tools, not a hard mystery. That comes next.
